# Add soul to writing

Give accurate prose a distinct voice without inventing experience or adding random stylistic noise.

## Establish the voice contract

Identify the goal, audience, channel, speaker, intended action, and required facts. Determine which changes the source and request permit:

- stronger expression of an existing stance
- an editorial stance that the source or request supplies
- first-person or conversational language
- more varied rhythm or less regular structure
- greater warmth, candor, humor, tension, or informality

Use supplied voice samples as the main guide. When a rewrite could misrepresent the speaker's beliefs or identity, ask before selecting a new stance. Otherwise, make the narrowest voice change that gives the prose a distinct voice.

Record the facts, quotations, chronology, uncertainty, obligations, instructions, and claims that must stay unchanged.

## Add voice deliberately

Use only the techniques that fit the speaker and channel:

- **State a view:** express the supplied stance instead of listing neutral pros and cons when the source supports a judgment
- **Show real tension:** acknowledge mixed effects, costs, doubts, or unresolved questions that the source supports
- **Use natural rhythm:** let emphasis and thought control sentence length; use a fragment, aside, or repeated phrase only when it has a clear purpose
- **Use first person carefully:** use `I` or `we` only when the speaker is known and the format permits it
- **Relax structure with intent:** remove perfect symmetry or an unnecessary section when a looser form improves the piece
- **Be concrete:** use supplied actions, numbers, examples, and consequences instead of generic feeling words

Do not apply every technique to every passage. A restrained voice can still be distinct.

## Protect truth and identity

Do not invent opinions, anecdotes, memories, direct experience, relationships, quotations, emotions, motives, evidence, or results. Do not claim an identity or membership that the speaker did not supply. Do not imitate dialect, age, culture, or social class from stereotypes.

Do not add mistakes, slang, fragments, irritation, or unusual punctuation only to make the text appear human. Keep uncertainty and disagreement at their source level. Follow legal, brand, accessibility, publication, and genre rules.

When the source material is thin, use restrained, specific language. Do not create a personal story or stance to fill the gap.
